=== intro ===
The last day of week 1, and the first problem where the constraints tell you the answer before you have thought of it.

> "No division" and "`O(n)`" together are not two constraints. They are a hint: whatever you need at position `i` has to be built from what is on its left and what is on its right.

=== problem ===
Return an array where `answer[i]` is the product of every element **except** `nums[i]`. No division, `O(n)` time.

```text
nums   = [1, 2, 3, 4]
answer = [24, 12, 8, 6]
```

Check one entry:

```text
answer[1] = 1 * 3 * 4 = 12   (everything but nums[1] = 2)
```

### Why "no division" is the whole problem

Without that constraint this is a two-liner: take the product of everything, then divide by each element.

```text
total = 1 * 2 * 3 * 4 = 24
answer[i] = 24 / nums[i]  ->  [24, 12, 8, 6]
```

That is `O(n)` and correct - until the array contains a zero. Then `total` is `0`, and `0 / 0` throws. Even handling zeros specially (count them; if exactly one, every other slot is `0` and the zero's slot is the product of the rest; if two or more, everything is `0`) works but is fiddly. The constraint exists to push you toward a solution that never has to care.

### The output array does not count

The problem asks for `O(1)` extra space *excluding the output*. That is standard: you have to return `n` values, so the result array is not "extra". Any additional array you allocate is.

=== pattern ===
**Prefix and Suffix products.**

Here is the reframe. The product of everything except `nums[i]` splits cleanly at `i`:

```text
answer[i] = (product of nums[0..i-1])  *  (product of nums[i+1..n-1])
             \_______ left ________/     \_______ right ________/
```

Neither half contains `nums[i]`. So if you knew, for every index, the product of everything to its left and the product of everything to its right, every answer is one multiplication.

And both of those are **running products** - each is the previous one times one more element. That makes each computable in a single pass.

### The picture

```text
nums    =  [ 1 ,  2 ,  3 ,  4 ]

left    =  [ 1 ,  1 ,  2 ,  6 ]     nothing to the left of index 0, so 1
right   =  [24 , 12 ,  4 ,  1 ]     nothing to the right of index 3, so 1

answer  =  [24 , 12 ,  8 ,  6 ]     left[i] * right[i]
```

The identity element `1` at each boundary is not a special case - it is the correct product of an empty set.

### Recognising it

Reach for prefix/suffix when:

- each answer depends on "everything before" and "everything after"
- the combining operation is associative (product, sum, min, max, GCD, OR)
- you are told not to use the inverse operation (division, subtraction)

The mental question:

> Can I split this at index `i` into a left part and a right part that never overlap?

=== bruteForce ===
For each `i`, multiply every other element.

### Dry run

```text
nums = [1, 2, 3, 4]
```

| i | elements multiplied | product | answer |
| -: | :------------------ | ------: | -----: |
| 0 | 2 * 3 * 4 | 24 | 24 |
| 1 | 1 * 3 * 4 | 12 | 12 |
| 2 | 1 * 2 * 4 | 8 | 8 |
| 3 | 1 * 2 * 3 | 6 | 6 |

Correct. Now count the work: 3 multiplications per row, 4 rows, 12 total. For `n` elements it is `n(n-1)`.

### Java

```java
class Solution {
    public int[] productExceptSelf(int[] nums) {

        int n = nums.length;
        int[] answer = new int[n];

        for (int i = 0; i < n; i++) {

            int product = 1;

            for (int j = 0; j < n; j++) {
                if (j != i) {
                    product *= nums[j];
                }
            }

            answer[i] = product;
        }

        return answer;
    }
}
```

### Complexity

**Time:** `O(n^2)` — **Space:** `O(1)` extra

### Where it hurts

Read the table's middle column downward:

```text
row 0:      2 * 3 * 4
row 1:  1 *     3 * 4
row 2:  1 * 2 *     4
row 3:  1 * 2 * 3
```

The product `3 * 4` is computed in row 0 and again in row 1. The product `1 * 2` is computed in row 2 and again in row 3. Every row recomputes a prefix and a suffix that a neighbouring row already worked out.

We are rebuilding the same partial products `n` times over. Compute each one **once** and the nested loop disappears.

=== optimization ===
Build the left products in one forward pass, then fold in the right products on a backward pass.

### Pass 1 - prefix products, written straight into the output

Walk left to right, carrying `prefix` (the product of everything strictly before `i`). Write it into `answer[i]`, then fold `nums[i]` in for the next position.

| i | prefix before write | `answer[i] = prefix` | prefix after `*= nums[i]` |
| -: | ------------------: | -------------------: | ------------------------: |
| 0 | 1 | `answer[0] = 1` | 1 * 1 = 1 |
| 1 | 1 | `answer[1] = 1` | 1 * 2 = 2 |
| 2 | 2 | `answer[2] = 2` | 2 * 3 = 6 |
| 3 | 6 | `answer[3] = 6` | 6 * 4 = 24 |

```text
answer = [1, 1, 2, 6]
```

Each slot now holds the product of everything to its **left**.

### Pass 2 - suffix products, multiplied in

Walk right to left, carrying `suffix` (the product of everything strictly after `i`). Multiply it into what is already there.

| i | suffix before | `answer[i]` before | `answer[i] *= suffix` | suffix after `*= nums[i]` |
| -: | ------------: | -----------------: | --------------------: | ------------------------: |
| 3 | 1 | 6 | 6 * 1 = **6** | 1 * 4 = 4 |
| 2 | 4 | 2 | 2 * 4 = **8** | 4 * 3 = 12 |
| 1 | 12 | 1 | 1 * 12 = **12** | 12 * 2 = 24 |
| 0 | 24 | 1 | 1 * 24 = **24** | 24 * 1 = 24 |

```text
answer = [24, 12, 8, 6]
```

### The two details that make this work

**Write before folding.** In pass 1 we assign `answer[i] = prefix` and only *then* do `prefix *= nums[i]`. Reverse those and `nums[i]` ends up included in its own answer - which is precisely what the problem excludes. The same ordering applies in pass 2.

**The output array is the scratch space.** Pass 1 leaves partial results in `answer`, and pass 2 completes them in place. That is what gets us to `O(1)` extra space - we never allocate a separate `left[]` and `right[]`.

### Why zeros need no special handling

```text
nums = [1, 0, 3]
```

Pass 1: `answer = [1, 1, 0]`  (prefix becomes 0 after index 1)
Pass 2: `answer[2] = 0*1 = 0`, `answer[1] = 1*3 = 3`, `answer[0] = 1*0 = 0`

```text
answer = [0, 3, 0]
```

Correct, with no zero-counting logic anywhere. The zero simply propagates into the prefixes and suffixes that legitimately contain it, and is absent from the one slot it should be absent from. This is the payoff for avoiding division.

=== complexity ===
### Time

Two passes over `n` elements, one multiplication each.

```text
O(n)
```

### Space

Two integers - `prefix` and `suffix`. The `answer` array is the required output, not extra space.

```text
O(1) extra
```

### Comparison

| Approach | Time | Extra space | Handles zeros? |
| :------- | ---: | ----------: | :------------- |
| Nested loops | `O(n^2)` | `O(1)` | yes |
| Division | `O(n)` | `O(1)` | only with special cases |
| Two `left[]`/`right[]` arrays | `O(n)` | `O(n)` | yes |
| Prefix + suffix in place | **`O(n)`** | **`O(1)`** | yes |

The intermediate version - building explicit `left[]` and `right[]` arrays - is worth mentioning in an interview as the step you thought through before collapsing them. It is easier to reason about and one observation away from the final answer.

=== implementation ===
```java
class Solution {
    public int[] productExceptSelf(int[] nums) {

        int n = nums.length;
        int[] answer = new int[n];

        // Pass 1: answer[i] = product of everything LEFT of i.
        int prefix = 1;
        for (int i = 0; i < n; i++) {
            answer[i] = prefix;     // write BEFORE folding nums[i] in
            prefix *= nums[i];
        }

        // Pass 2: multiply in the product of everything RIGHT of i.
        int suffix = 1;
        for (int i = n - 1; i >= 0; i--) {
            answer[i] *= suffix;    // again, multiply BEFORE folding
            suffix *= nums[i];
        }

        return answer;
    }
}
```

### The lines that matter

**1.**

```java
answer[i] = prefix;     // write BEFORE folding nums[i] in
prefix *= nums[i];
```

The order is the correctness condition. `prefix` holds the product of `nums[0..i-1]` at the moment of the write; folding `nums[i]` in first would make it `nums[0..i]` and include the element we are meant to exclude.

**2.**

```java
int prefix = 1;
```

`1`, not `0`. It is the multiplicative identity, and it is also the genuinely correct product of the empty set to the left of index 0.

**3.**

```java
answer[i] *= suffix;
```

`*=`, not `=`. Pass 2 completes what pass 1 started; overwriting would discard the prefix half.

### Why two loops and not one

You cannot know the suffix product at index `i` until you have seen every element to its right, so the passes must run in opposite directions. Two `O(n)` passes is still `O(n)`.

### Java notes

- `new int[n]` is zero-filled, but every slot is assigned in pass 1 before it is read, so that does not matter here.
- Products can overflow `int` on large inputs. LeetCode guarantees every prefix and suffix product fits in a 32-bit integer; without that guarantee, use `long`.

=== edgeCases ===
### Contains one zero

```text
[1, 0, 3]  ->  [0, 3, 0]
```

Only the zero's own slot gets a non-zero answer, because it is the only product that excludes the zero. Handled naturally.

### Contains two or more zeros

```text
[0, 2, 0]  ->  [0, 0, 0]
```

Every slot's product includes at least one of the zeros, so every answer is `0`. Again no special case: pass 1 gives `[1, 0, 0]`, pass 2 multiplies by suffixes `[0, 0, 1]`.

### All zeros

```text
[0, 0]  ->  [0, 0]
```

### Negative numbers

```text
[-1, 2, -3]  ->  [-6, 3, -2]
```

Signs multiply through correctly. `answer[0] = 2 * -3 = -6`. Nothing about the algorithm cares about sign.

### Two elements

```text
[2, 3]  ->  [3, 2]
```

Pass 1: `[1, 2]`. Pass 2: `answer[1] = 2*1 = 2`, `answer[0] = 1*3 = 3`. The smallest interesting case, and the one where the boundary `1`s do all the work.

### Contains a one

```text
[1, 1, 1]  ->  [1, 1, 1]
```

### Large values

With `n = 10^5` the intermediate `prefix` can overflow an `int` if the values are large. LeetCode constrains the input so every partial product fits; outside that guarantee, widen `prefix` and `suffix` to `long` and cast on write.

=== takeaway ===
> When `answer[i]` depends on **everything before** and **everything after** `i`, build a prefix pass and a suffix pass. Two `O(n)` walks replace the nested loop.

### The core observation

`answer[i] = left[i] * right[i]`, and neither side contains `nums[i]`. Both sides are running products, so each costs one multiplication per element. The output array can hold the left halves while the right halves are folded in, which is what makes the extra space constant.

### The pattern trigger

```text
       answer[i] needs "all but i"
                    |
                    v
        split at i:  left | i | right
                    |
                    v
      pass 1 ->  write running LEFT product
      pass 2 <-  multiply in running RIGHT product
                    |
                    v
      identity (1 for product, 0 for sum) at both boundaries
```

### The common mistakes

**Folding before writing.** `prefix *= nums[i]` above `answer[i] = prefix` includes `nums[i]` in its own answer. It produces plausible-looking output and is wrong everywhere.

**Reaching for division.** It works on the sample input and dies on the first zero. The constraint is there precisely to stop you.

**Allocating `left[]` and `right[]` and calling it `O(1)`.** Two extra arrays is `O(n)` extra space. It is a fine intermediate step to describe out loud, but say honestly what it costs.

### What to remember in six months

> Prefix/suffix is the answer whenever a per-index result needs context from both directions and the combining operation has no usable inverse.

The same skeleton solves running-sum range queries, Trapping Rain Water (prefix max and suffix max), Candy, and any "except self" variant with sum, min, max, GCD or XOR swapped in for product. Only the identity element changes.
